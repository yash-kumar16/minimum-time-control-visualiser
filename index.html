How to use this tool

1. Choose a system. Either pick a preset (past exam question) or type your own a, b values.

2. Read the classification. The panel shows eigenvalues, rest points, and the type of singular point. When a² + 4b > 0 you get nodes or saddles (real eigenvalues); when a² + 4b < 0 you get spirals or centres (complex eigenvalues).

3. Understand the phase plane. Red trajectories orbit the u=+1 rest point, blue trajectories orbit the u=−1 rest point. For real eigenvalues these are parabolas (nodes) or hyperbolas (saddles); for complex eigenvalues they are spirals (focus) or ellipses (centre). The green curve is the switching curve. Shaded green area is the controllable region.

4. Click to simulate. Click a phase-plane point to explore a candidate bang-bang path towards the origin. Curve matching is approximate; the resulting path is not a certified minimum-time solution. The solid purple arc is a constant-control segment and the dashed segment follows an approximate backward-flow curve. The two may not join exactly.

5. Toggle elements. Use checkboxes to show/hide different parts of the diagram — useful for building up the sketch step by step in class.
